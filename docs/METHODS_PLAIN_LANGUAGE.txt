DRY-PERIOD STREAMFLOW METHOD IN PLAIN LANGUAGE
================================================

PURPOSE
-------

Rivers usually decline when detected precipitation and proxy snowmelt are
absent, but the decline is not perfectly smooth. This study measured the daily
departures around the expected decline, determined how their size changes with
flow, tested how often unusually large departures occur, and carried those
measurements into conditional low-flow prediction.

The source inventory contained 9,067 CONUS gages. Quality control retained
5,227 basins. The primary variance exponent was estimable for 3,727 basins from
9,199,635 screened one-day transitions during 1980–2025.


1. IDENTIFY CANDIDATE DRY-PERIOD TRANSITIONS
---------------------------------------------

Daily USGS discharge was matched with basin-average precipitation and
temperature. Temperature was used to construct a proxy indicator of possible
snowmelt. The proxy was a screen, not a reconstruction of snow water
equivalent.

For a transition beginning on day t and ending τ days later:

    D(t,τ)
      = 1{ maximum P(s) ≤ P₀ }
        × 1{ Σ M(s) = 0 },

    for s = t-a+1, ..., t+τ.

P(s) is basin-average precipitation, P₀ = 0.1 mm/day, a = 3 days, and
M(s) is the binary proxy-snowmelt-risk indicator. The window includes the
three-day period ending on the starting day and continues through the future
endpoint.

A retained transition also required a continuous screened run of at least five
days, consecutive dates, and positive flow at both endpoints. Missing forcing
excluded the transition. The screen identifies periods with no detected
precipitation or proxy snowmelt; it does not exclude every unmeasured input,
regulation effect, or observation error. Both falling and rising changes were
retained because keeping only declines would change the measured variance.


2. PUT DIFFERENT RIVERS ON A COMPARABLE SCALE
----------------------------------------------

Discharge was converted to basin runoff depth:

    Q_mm(i,t) = 2.446575545 × Q_cfs(i,t) / A(i),

where A(i) is drainage area in square kilometers and Q_mm is in millimeters
per day.

Each basin was then divided by its own positive-flow median:

    q(i,t)
      = Q_mm(i,t)
        / median{ Q_mm(i,s) : Q_mm(i,s) > 10⁻⁶ mm/day }.

On this scale, q = 2 means that current flow is twice the basin's normalization
median. The forward change was

    Δq(i,t;τ) = q(i,t+τ) - q(i,t).

A negative Δq is a decline; a positive Δq is an increase.


3. ESTIMATE THE EXPECTED CHANGE AND ITS VARIANCE
-------------------------------------------------

Within each basin, transitions were placed into equal-frequency starting-flow
bins. The primary one-day calculation used 20 bins, required at least 50
transitions in a retained bin, and required at least six valid bins for a
basin-level fit.

For a flow bin centered on q, the conditional mean-change rate was

    μΔ(q,τ)
      = E[ Δq(τ) | q(t)=q, D(t,τ)=1 ] / τ.

The conditional increment variance was

    S²(q,τ)
      = Var[ Δq(τ) | q(t)=q, D(t,τ)=1 ].

Plain meaning: μΔ describes the expected change from the current flow state.
S² describes the spread of individual changes around that expectation. The
mean was removed before calculating the variance.


4. FIT THE BASIN-SPECIFIC VARIANCE LAW
---------------------------------------

The relation between starting flow and conditional variance was fitted as

    S²(q,τ) = cτ × q^(mτ),       cτ > 0,

or, equivalently,

    log[S²(q,τ)] = log(cτ) + mτ × log(q).

cτ controls the overall magnitude of uncertainty. mτ tells how rapidly the
variance changes with starting flow. The primary estimate used τ = 1 day.

    m = 0   variance is constant across flow states
    m = 2   variance grows as q², so standard deviation is proportional to q

Values above or below two indicate stronger or weaker flow dependence.

The equivalent local Box-Cox parameter was

    λ* = 1 - m/2.

λ* = 0 corresponds to the logarithmic transformation. This is a local
variance-stabilizing approximation for finite changes, not an exact
transformation for every possible increment.

The executable routine stores

    D₂(q,τ) = S²(q,τ) / (2τ).

At a fixed lead, dividing by 2τ changes the coefficient but not the exponent or
the standardized departures. The paper reports the more readily interpreted S²
relation.


5. REMOVE THE CHANGING SCALE AND MEASURE EXTREMES
--------------------------------------------------

For each retained one-day change, the fitted mean was removed and the remainder
was divided by the fitted conditional standard deviation:

    z(i,t)
      = [ Δq(i,t;1) - μ_hat_i(q(i,t)) ]
        / sqrt[ c_hat_i × q(i,t)^(m_hat_i) ].

The resulting values were centered and rescaled within each basin to have mean
zero and standard deviation one.

Plain meaning: z asks how unusual a change was after accounting for both the
expected change and the uncertainty appropriate for its starting flow.

Three-standard-deviation event frequencies were compared with the Gaussian
expectation:

    T(i)       = P_i(|z| > 3) / [2 Φ(-3)]
    T_plus(i)  = P_i(z > 3)   / Φ(-3)
    T_minus(i) = P_i(z < -3)  / Φ(-3)

Φ is the standard-normal cumulative distribution function. A tail multiple of
one agrees with a Gaussian model. A value of five means that the event occurred
five times as often as the Gaussian model predicts.


6. TEST A CLASSICAL RECESSION-THEORY EXPLANATION
-------------------------------------------------

A separate analysis used matched declining transitions. If recession follows

    Δq = -k × q^b,

then

    Var(Δq | q) = Var(k | q) × q^(2b).

If variability in recession rate k is independent of flow, the decline-only
variance exponent should satisfy

    m_d = 2b.

The analysis tested that assumption directly with

    Var(k | q) = d × q^γ,

which implies

    m_d = 2b + γ.

γ = 0 denotes flow-independent recession-rate variance. γ < 0 means that
recession-rate variability narrows as flow rises. The identity is exact for
infinitesimally narrow flow bins; finite-bin fits can differ slightly. This
theory test interprets the observed scaling and is not an input to prediction.


7. CONSTRUCT AND TEST CONDITIONAL LOW-FLOW PREDICTIONS
-------------------------------------------------------

Prediction models used only records ending on 31 December 2015 for fitting.
Flow was renormalized by each basin's positive pre-2016 median. Each lead was
fitted directly rather than obtained by repeatedly applying a one-day model.
Evaluation used 2016–2025 data.

Noisy basin exponents were regularized toward m = 2:

    m_tilde
      = [ m_hat/s_m² + 2/(0.75)² ]
        / [ 1/s_m² + 1/(0.75)² ],

and then bounded to [0,4]. Here s_m is the estimated standard error of m_hat.

For each eligible starting state, 101 deterministic endpoint members were
generated:

    q(i,t+h,j)
      = max{ 0,
             q(i,t)
             + δ_hat(i,h,q_tilde)
             + sqrt[ c_hat(i,h) × q_tilde^(m_tilde(i,h)) ]
               × z(i,g,j) },

    j = 1, ..., 101.

q_tilde is starting flow clipped to the range supported by the training data,
and z(i,g,j) is residual quantile j from pre-2016 starting-flow group g. The
equation adds the expected change and a flow-dependent departure to current
flow. The variance law sets the spread; the residual quantiles set the shape.

Three residual models were compared:

    1. one basin-wide Gaussian distribution;
    2. Gaussian distributions in five starting-flow groups; and
    3. empirical residual quantiles in the same five flow groups.

The first comparison isolates remaining flow-state dependence. The second
isolates non-Gaussian shape after group means and variances are matched. The
comparisons have different references and are not additive.

Complete endpoint distributions were evaluated with

    CRPS(F,y) = E|X-y| - 0.5 × E|X-X'|,

where X and X' are independent draws from the predicted distribution F.

Probabilities of falling below fixed pre-2016 20th-, 10th-, and 5th-percentile
flow thresholds were evaluated with

    BS = (1/N) × Σ from n=1 to N of (p_n - y_n)².

Lower scores are better. Improvement was measured as

    Gain = Score(reference) - Score(structured).

Positive gain favors the structured model; percentage reductions divide the
gain by the corresponding reference score.

Every prediction result conditions on observed continuation of screened dry
conditions through the endpoint. The method predicts the endpoint distribution
under that condition; it does not forecast whether the dry spell continues.
